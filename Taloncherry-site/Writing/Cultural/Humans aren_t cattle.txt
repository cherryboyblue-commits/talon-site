Comparing our healthcare system to a cattle ranch can provide an interesting perspective on the structure, organization, and dynamics of the healthcare industry. While there are clear differences between the two, there are some parallels that can be drawn to illustrate certain aspects of the healthcare system that are failing and failing hard, in my opinion, specifically for these similarities. My parents are both victims of this system, and I am quickly approaching the same fate, as is everyone in this country. Below are my top five similarities that I will discuss in this article.

1. Profit-Driven Motives:
In both the healthcare system and a cattle ranch, profit-driven motives often take precedence over the well-being of individuals. In the healthcare system, the pursuit of financial gains can lead to inflated medical costs, over utilization of services, and a focus on maximizing revenue similar to the way a cattle rancher may prioritize maximizing economic gains over the welfare of the animals. The dangers of this reside in the significant implications it has for patient care, public health, and the overall well-being of individuals. When financial interests take precedence over the delivery of high-quality, patient-centered care, a range of issues can arise, including inflated medical costs, and over utilization of services.
When profit-driven motives influence healthcare delivery, patient care is compromised. Healthcare providers and institutions face pressures to maximize revenue leading to unnecessary procedures, and a focus on quantity over quality of care. In such a system, the emphasis shifts from providing comprehensive, evidence-based care to meeting financial targets, potentially resulting in sub optimal outcomes for patients. Furthermore, the pursuit of profit alone leads to cost-cutting measures that compromise the quality of care. For example, healthcare facilities have reduced staffing levels, limited access to essential resources, and prioritize cost-saving measures over patient safety as was the case in the COVID vaccine, and many other poorly tested profit driven medications pushed by doctors, and hospitals for large monetary incentives. Such decisions have had dire consequences for patient outcomes and have contributed to a decline in their overall quality of care.
This raises serious ethical concerns related to the core principles of medical practice. The Hippocratic Oath, a foundational ethical code for physicians, emphasizes the primary obligation to prioritize the well-being of patients. However, profit-driven motives have created conflicts of interest, where financial considerations have influenced medical decision-making, compromising the integrity of the patient-physician relationship. Additionally, this model has incentivized the over prescription of medications, unnecessary tests, and procedures, driven by financial incentives rather than clinical necessity. One very glaring example of this is trans elective surgeries being performed on minors under the guise of care, and the prescription of puberty blockers for gender dysphoria with irreversible affects. This practice raises ethical questions regarding the appropriateness of care, patient autonomy, and the potential for harm resulting from unnecessary medical interventions.
The pursuit of profit in healthcare often leads to inflated medical costs, placing a significant financial burden on patients and their families. High out-of-pocket expenses, copayments, and deductibles can result in financial hardship, forcing individuals to make difficult choices between accessing necessary medical care and meeting other basic needs. In cases where individuals are unable to afford essential healthcare services, the consequences are obviously detrimental to their health and well-being. Moreover, this can lead to the commodification of patients, where individuals are viewed as sources of revenue rather than recipients of care. This perspective leads to practices that prioritize profitability over ensuring proper, and quality care.
2. Commodification of Patients:
The dehumanization of patients in the healthcare system, where individuals are often treated as commodities rather than as sentient beings deserving of empathy and personalized care, mirrors the approach to livestock on a cattle ranch. Losing sight of the individual's, God instilled, intrinsic human value and dignity, leads again to a focus on quantity over quality of care or treatment based on numbers, and paper. This poses several significant dangers that have far-reaching implications for both patients and the healthcare industry as a whole. Patience, or the ability to wait calmly in the face of frustration, is an essential virtue in healthcare, as it allows for thorough and thoughtful decision-making, the development of strong patient-provider relationships, and the delivery of high-quality care. However, the current system's focus on efficiency on paper often undermines the practice and cultivation of patience, leading to a range of negative consequences.
These practices in the healthcare system have led to a culture of immediate gratification and quick fixes, which has compromised the quality of patient care. Healthcare providers and institutions have prioritized speed and volume over thoroughness and careful consideration. This has resulted in rushed diagnoses, hasty treatment decisions, and a lack of attention to the holistic needs of patients. Patients often receive unnecessary tests and procedures, or worse no tests or procedures when they are needed. They are prescribed medications without sufficient consideration of their individual circumstances, and experience inadequate communication and education about their conditions. The pressure to see more patients in less time erodes the practice of patience in healthcare, leading to sub optimal outcomes and extremely harmful consequences.
The commodification of patients strains the doctor-patient relationship, which is a cornerstone of effective healthcare delivery. Patients often rely on healthcare providers not only for medical expertise but also for empathy, understanding, and support. However, in a system that prioritizes speed-based efficiency on paper, and in practice, healthcare professionals may find it challenging to dedicate the time and attention necessary to build meaningful connections with their patients. Rushed appointments, limited face-to-face interaction, and reliance on electronic health records all contribute to a sense of detachment and impersonal care, undermining the patient's trust and confidence in their healthcare providers. This erosion of the doctor-patient relationship has, in my opinion, had the most profound negative impact on patient satisfaction, treatment adherence, and health outcomes.
Furthermore, this takes a toll on the well-being of healthcare professionals themselves. Physicians, nurses, and other healthcare providers are often under immense pressure to meet productivity targets, navigate complex administrative requirements, and address the needs of a large number of patients within limited time frames. This high-stress environment leads to burnout, and decreased job satisfaction, ultimately compromising the quality of care they are able to provide on both a physical and emotional level. Additionally, the erosion of patience in healthcare has contributed to a culture of impatience and frustration among staff, leading to interpersonal conflicts, communication breakdowns, and decreased collaboration, all of which have negatively impacted the work environment and thus patient care.
3. Emphasis on Pharmaceutical Solutions:
The over reliance on pharmaceutical interventions in the healthcare system, often at the expense of holistic, preventive care, shares similarities with the agricultural industry's reliance on medications and growth-promoting substances to maintain the health and productivity of livestock. We have created a system with an emphasis on treating symptoms or conditions with medications rather than focusing on comprehensive, patient-centered care based on lifestyle, and other genetic or circumstantial causes of illness. The emphasis on pharmaceutical solutions in the United States healthcare system has brought both benefits and dangers. While pharmaceuticals have undoubtedly revolutionized the treatment of many diseases and improved the quality of life for countless individuals, an over reliance on pharmaceutical interventions has led to a range of significant dangers for patients, healthcare providers, and the healthcare system as a whole.
One of the primary dangers of this is the potential for over medication and the associated risks of adverse drug reactions, dependency, and misuse. The United States has seen a significant increase in the prescribing and consumption of pharmaceuticals, often driven by direct-to-consumer advertising, aggressive marketing by pharmaceutical companies, and a healthcare system that incentivizes the use of medications as a primary treatment modality. As a result, patients have been prescribed medications that they do not necessarily need, leading to unnecessary side effects and complications. The overuse of certain classes of medications, such as opioids and antibiotics, has contributed to the rise of opioid addiction and antibiotic resistance, both of which pose serious public health challenges.
The neglect of non-pharmacological interventions and holistic approaches to patient care has been, in my opinion, a black mark on healthcare as a whole in this nation. Many conditions and health issues can be effectively managed or alleviated through lifestyle modifications, behavioral therapies, physical rehabilitation, and other non-pharmacological interventions. However, in a system that prioritizes quick fixes, these non-pharmacological approaches have been underutilized and or overlooked, depriving patients of potentially beneficial and less invasive treatment options. This has perpetuated a cycle of medication dependency and contributed to the underdevelopment of comprehensive, multidisciplinary care models that address the diverse needs of patients.
The emphasis on pharmaceutical solutions also has significant financial implications, contributing to rising healthcare costs and economic burden for patients, healthcare providers, and payers. The high cost of many prescription medications, particularly specialty drugs and biologics, has led to financial strain for patients, limited access to essential treatments, and increased out-of-pocket expenses. Additionally, the influence of pharmaceutical companies on drug pricing, formulary decisions, and prescription patterns has contributed to the escalating cost of healthcare and has strained public and private healthcare budgets. As a result, this has raised concerns about the affordability and sustainability of healthcare delivery, particularly for individuals with chronic conditions who require ongoing pharmaceutical treatments.
Another danger of these overused, and under researched solutions is the potential for medicalization of normal human conditions and the promotion of "disease mongering." Pharmaceutical marketing and industry influence have been known to expand the definition of certain medical conditions, create new diagnostic categories, and promote the use of medications for issues that may not necessarily require pharmaceutical intervention at all. This trend has led to the unnecessary medicalization of normal variations in human health, pathologizing natural experiences and emotions. Disease mongering has contributed to the over diagnosis and over treatment of conditions, leading to unnecessary healthcare expenditures, potential harm from medications, and a shift away from addressing the underlying social, environmental, and behavioral determinants of health.
4. Inadequate Emphasis on Preventive Care:
The reactive approach to healthcare, characterized by a focus on treating acute conditions rather than prioritizing preventive care, parallels the approach to livestock management on a cattle ranch. Our healthcare system lacks sufficient emphasis on preventive measures that reduce the burden of disease and improve overall well-being. The inadequate emphasis on preventive care in the United States healthcare system poses numerous dangers that have profound implications for public health, healthcare costs, and the well-being of individuals and communities. Preventive care, including screenings, tested and proven vaccinations, lifestyle counseling, and early interventions, plays a crucial role in reducing the burden of disease, improving health outcomes, and mitigating the economic and social costs associated with untreated or advanced illnesses. However, the current healthcare system often falls short in prioritizing and promoting preventive care, leading to a range of significant dangers.
One of the primary dangers here is the increased burden of preventable diseases and chronic conditions. Many of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality in the United States, such as heart disease, cancer, diabetes, and respiratory illnesses, are closely linked to modifiable risk factors, including poor diet, physical inactivity, tobacco use, and excessive alcohol consumption. By not prioritizing preventive care, the healthcare system misses valuable opportunities to address these risk factors early, leading to a higher prevalence of preventable diseases and the associated human suffering, disability, and premature death. Admittedly much of this is due to the free choice of the patients, but the forementioned undermining of doctor patient relations plays a large part in the patients lack of trust in their doctor, and the doctors inability to have these discussions with their patients.
Ignoring preventive care contributes to the escalation of healthcare costs and the economic burden associated with managing advanced or late-stage illnesses. Preventive interventions, such as screenings, immunizations, and lifestyle counseling, are often cost-effective in the long term, as they can avert the need for expensive treatments, hospitalizations, and long-term care for preventable conditions. However, when preventive care is underutilized, individuals experience delayed diagnoses, progression of diseases to more advanced stages, and increased healthcare utilization, leading to higher costs for both patients and the healthcare system.
The inadequate emphasis on preventive care contributes to a reactive rather than proactive approach to health, leading to missed opportunities for early interventions and health promotion. The healthcare system often focuses on acute care and the management of existing conditions, rather than investing in strategies to prevent the onset of illnesses or to identify health risks at an early stage. This reactive approach results in missed opportunities to address risk factors and to empower individuals to make informed choices about their health, leading to poorer health outcomes and a higher burden of chronic conditions over time.
In addition, this undermines the concept of patient-centered care and the promotion of holistic well-being. Preventive care encompasses a wide range of services aimed at promoting not only physical health but also mental, emotional, and social well-being. By neglecting preventive interventions that address mental health, healthy behavior promotion, and social determinants of health, the healthcare system fails to adequately support individuals in achieving overall wellness. This can result in a fragmented approach to care, leading to care that prioritizes healthcare as a business rather than a system designed to improve the wellbeing of humanity.
5. Influence of Lobbying and Special Interests:
The influence of lobbying and special interests in shaping healthcare policy and regulation has drawn comparisons to the agricultural industry's influence on government policies related to food production and animal welfare. In our healthcare system, vested interests have shaped regulations and policies in ways that prioritize profit margins over the well-being of individuals. This has far-reaching and dangerous significance, impacting healthcare policy, patient care, healthcare costs, and public health outcomes. Lobbying and special interests, often driven by the profit motives of various stakeholders, including pharmaceutical companies, insurance providers, and other industry players, can shape healthcare legislation, regulations, and decision-making, leading to a range of detrimental effects.
One of the primary dangers of special interests in the healthcare system is the potential distortion of healthcare policy and regulation to serve narrow industry interests rather than the public good. Lobbying activities, including campaign contributions, advocacy efforts, and direct lobbying of legislators and policymakers, has led to the prioritization of industry-friendly policies at the expense of broader public health goals. This has manifested in various ways, such as the relaxation of regulatory oversight, favorable tax treatment for certain industry segments, and the blocking or dilution of legislation aimed at reducing healthcare costs and enhancing patient safety.
The influence of lobbying and special interests has led to the perpetuation of a fragmented and inefficient healthcare system, characterized by a lack of transparency, excessive administrative complexity, and barriers to competition and innovation. Industry stakeholders have advocated for policies that protect their market positions, stifle competition, and maintain opaque pricing practices, all of which contribute to the perpetuation of a healthcare system that is less responsive to consumer needs and more focused on maximizing profits for vested interests.
These influences pose significant dangers to patient care and public health outcomes. Industry-driven policies and regulations influence clinical practice guidelines, formulary decisions, and coverage determinations, leading to the over utilization of certain treatments, drugs, and technologies, and the underutilization of others. This compromises the quality and appropriateness of care, leading to sub optimal health outcomes, unnecessary costs, and harm to patients. The influence of special interests has impeded public health efforts with false nutrition initiatives, and environmental health regulations, by undermining evidence-based public health interventions that conflict with industry interests.
In addition, the influence of lobbying and special interests contributes to the escalation of healthcare costs and the perpetuation of a system marked by what could only be described as wasteful efficiency. Industry stakeholders may advocate for policies that protect their profit margins, such as inflated drug prices, reimbursement rates, or administrative fees, leading to higher healthcare costs for patients, employers, and taxpayers. Moreover, the influence of special interests can obstruct efforts to implement cost-containment measures, promote value-based care, and address systemic inefficiencies, resulting in the perpetuation of a healthcare system that is characterized by high costs and suboptimal value for patients and society.
The influence of lobbying and special interests can also erode public trust in the healthcare system and the integrity of healthcare institutions. When the public perceives that healthcare policy decisions are unduly influenced by special interests, it can undermine confidence in the fairness, transparency, and accountability of the healthcare system. This erosion of trust can have profound implications for patient-provider relationships, public health initiatives, and the willingness of individuals to engage with the healthcare system, potentially leading to decreased compliance with public health recommendations especially when those public health regulations blatantly promulgate the opposite of healthcare.
To say the least:
Eliminating profit in healthcare is not the answer, but prioritizing profit, volume, drugs, and the people that benefit off illness over the people that are ill is a model hardly suitable for cattle let alone the human race. I see one solution. Healthcare should be as personal as the small family ranch. They know the names of their cows, and they treat them well for the entirety of their life. Preventative care and emotional connection are essential to the health and wellbeing of both the farmer and the cattle. It is a symbiotic relationship that eliminates the stress on the animal and saves the poor farmer money. The creation of the large, centralized cattle ranches has all but destroyed the small family ranches, and thus resulted in the inhumane care of these animals. That fate is shared by the medical industry, and many other industries and institutions today. It is even shared by the church, undermining the trust needed to sustain it. It bares a remarkable resemblance to communism which is really just a monopoly on the one resource we all take for granted, people, and the elimination of God, the source of our humanity, and the reason healthcare even exists at all. God is the solution to all suffering. Healthcare without God is just business.
